Walmart · Inventory Intelligence

Demand has a calendar, not an economy.

Three years of weekly sales across 45 stores show unemployment, weather, and price levels barely move the needle. What moves it is Thanksgiving week — and knowing exactly which stores to stock, and how much.

Every macro variable sits within ±0.11 of zero — unemployment, temperature and CPI are effectively noise at the chain level. Even the holiday flag alone is weak here; its power only shows up once you split holidays out by name (next panel).

Sales lift by specific holiday

% above an average non-holiday week
Thanksgiving week runs +41% — the single biggest event of the year. The flagged “Christmas” week (w/e Dec 31) actually dips below average: the real rush happens in the unflagged days just before it.

05 — Actions

Recommendations for inventory planning

  • ABuild the sharpest inventory buffer specifically around Thanksgiving week — and treat the two weeks before Dec 31, not that week itself, as the real Christmas peak.
  • BSize orders off each store's 12-week forecast and confidence band, not one chain-wide number — performance varies up to 8x between stores.
  • CRun High-Volume stores leaner (predictable demand); give Mid-Volume stores extra holiday stock (largest lift); give Low-Volume stores a bigger relative safety buffer.
  • DWatch stores #38 and #44 for local unemployment swings — both are far more exposed than the rest of the chain.
  • ERe-fit forecasts quarterly as new weeks land; Holt-Winters accuracy depends on the seasonal pattern holding.
